    1. Food first videos
        - *We stopped doing takeout (Madilynn)*
            
            **Hook (first 2-3 seconds):**
            "We haven't ordered takeout in [X weeks]." Dead serious. Let it land. 
            Then show clips of various plated food. "This is what we've been eating."
            
            **The setup:**
            Audience assumes meal prep, effort, a whole thing. Quick montage: different nights, different dishes, all looking good. No explanation yet. Just the food.
            
            **The reveal:**
            "I should probably show you why." Cut to Posha already on the counter doing its thing. No introduction, no demo voice. There it is. The audience figures it out. Have partner/kid looking at it. Maybe a couple comments in back ground “that smell is making me hungry”
            
            **The cook:**
            Food coming together. You nearby, not cooking. You could be even doing other things while the cooking happens.   
            
            **The taste:**
            Family at the table. Real reactions.
            
            **The punchline:**
            "Yeah. We're not going back."
            
        - Here’s everything we ate last week. But I did not cook or order out. (*Erika*)
            
            **Hook (first 2-3 seconds):**
            "Here's everything we ate last week." Start the food montage immediately. Dish after dish, looks good, looks real.
            
            **The turn:**
            "I didn't cook any of it." Pause. "Didn't order out either."
            
            Let that sit. Audience is confused. That's exactly where you want them.
            
            **The reveal:**
            Cut to Posha on the counter. No explanation needed yet. Just show it running. The food montage already did the work.
            
            **The rest:**
            Keep it short. Maybe one dish being plated, family eating, real reaction. You're just... there. Relaxed.
            
            **The punchline:**
            "Best week of my life."

        - "The Talk" (New Nicole or new creator)

            "Babe. I need to talk to you about something."

            "I'm done cooking."
            Dead serious. Let the partner react. Don't break.

            Walk them to the kitchen. Posha is on the counter, already going. Let the partner take it in.

            Partner goes through the stages. Confusion, then curiosity, then the smell or the food comes out and something shifts.
            
            **The punchline:**
            Partner takes a bite. Looks at her. "...okay you can stop cooking."

### Jacob

- Idea 1: 10 kids in 10 years: What do you want for dinner tonight?
    
    **"What do you want for dinner tonight?"**
    
    **Hook (first 2-3 seconds):**
    
    Text overlay: "10 kids. 10 different dinner orders. 1 robot."
    
    All 10 kids shout out what they want simultaneously — pure chaos, overlapping voices, the camera capturing the mayhem.
    
    **The moment:**
    
    One of the younger kids breaks from the group, steps forward, and insists on their dish. Loud, stubborn, the way only a little kid can be. The parent (Jacob) looks at the camera, shrugs: "Alright, we're making [dish]."
    
    **The product:**
    
    Cut to Posha. Close-up shots of the robot actually cooking the dish — ingredients going in, the machine working, steam, the food coming out. This is where the audience sees the product for the first time. No voiceover explanation of what Posha is. The visuals do the work — it's a robot, it's cooking, it's real.
    
    **The payoff:**
    
    Cut to multiple kids sitting together, eating the dish, reacting. Honest faces — who loves it, who's skeptical, who goes back for more.
    
    The youngest kid who demanded the dish gets the last shot.
    
    ---
    
    **Why the structure works:**
    
    - **Chaos → single voice → robot → reactions** is a clean four-beat arc that fits in under 60 seconds
    - The younger kid insisting on their dish gives the video a character and a mini-story — the audience follows that kid through to the payoff
    - **Production note:** The Posha cooking shots matter a lot here. They need to look good — tight framing, the food actually looking appetizing.
- Idea 2 (Reusing some bit of who’s the better cook) (to be published later)
    
    **"Proving Them Wrong" — Cassanda vs. Posha**
    
    **Hook: The scoreboard of who cooks best**
    
    Text on screen, no faces: "We asked 10 kids who cooks better — Mom or the robot." Beat. The number appears: "7 said the robot." Cut to Cassanda in the kitchen staring at Posha on the counter. "Okay."
    
    **Full Video Outline (~45-60 seconds)**
    
    **Beat 1 — The provocation (0:00–0:05):**
    
    The hook. One or two quick cuts of kids saying "Posha" cherry-picked from existing lineup footage. Into Cassanda's reaction.
    
    **Beat 2 — The challenge (0:05–0:10):**
    
    Cassanda to camera, short and competitive: "Alright. Same dish. Me vs. the robot. The kids decide." No need to explain what Posha is — the audience is about to see it.
    
    **Beat 3 — The cook (0:10–0:30):**
    
    Intercut Cassanda cooking and Posha cooking. Cassanda chopping, stirring, tasting her own food, looking confident. Posha doing its thing — close-ups of the robot working, ingredients, steam, food coming together. This is the product visibility section. Posha gets 10-15 seconds of screen time, doing real cooking, looking good.
    
    **Beat 4 — The blind test (0:30–0:40):**
    
    Two plates. Kids don't know which is which. Quick cuts of kids tasting both — reactions, faces, deliberation.
    
    **Beat 5 — The verdict (0:40–0:50):**
    
    Kids vote. Show of hands or quick cuts of each kid pointing at a plate. Reveal which plate was Posha's, which was Cassanda's.
    
    **Beat 6 — The reaction (0:50–0:55):**
    
    Cassanda's genuine response. If she loses: humor in a mom being humbled by a machine. If she wins: redemption. Either outcome works.
    
    **How existing footage gets reused**
    
    The lineup footage becomes Beat 1 only — a 3-5 second montage at the top. Cherry-pick the best 2-3 kid answers (funniest, most confident "Posha" moments) as the cold open. The full lineup is not published as its own video — the best moments are extracted as the inciting incident for this one.
    

### Madilynn

- Opening Idea - **"Mom vs. Impossible Dish"**
    
    **Hook (first 2-3 seconds):**
    
    The kid or wife challenges Madi with an ambitious dish — something she'd normally never attempt. Madi doesn't hesitate, looks at camera: "EASY."
    
    **The secret:**
    
    Kid leaves the room. Madi drops to a whisper, leans into the camera like she's letting the audience in on something her kid can't know.
    
    She pulls Posha into frame and quietly explains what it is and how it works. A glance over her shoulder to make sure the kid isn't coming back. The audience is now her co-conspirator.
    
    **The** **cook:**
    
    Posha does its thing — close-ups of the robot cooking, food coming together, steam. Madi is nearby but not doing the work. On her phone, hanging out with the kid, doing anything except cooking. She's getting credit for work she's not doing and the audience knows it.
    
    **The taste:**
    
    Food comes out. Madi plates it like she made it herself, brings it to the table with full pride. The family tries it. Real reactions, especially from the kid.
    
    **The punchline:**
    
    "Told you. Easy." She either keeps the secret — a knowing look at the camera while the family praises her — or someone catches on. Both are funny, and the audience was in on it the whole time.
    
- Idea 2/3 - Mom Vs Robot (not the first video)
    
    **"Mom vs. Robot" — @themadivlog (Later Video)**
    
    **Hook (first 2-3 seconds):**
    
    The kid sitting at the table, blindfolded. Two plates in front of her. She can't see anything. Madi's voice from off-camera: "One of these was made by me. One was made by a robot." The kid grins under the blindfold — she's excited, a little nervous. The audience immediately knows what's happening: a test. 
    
    They want to see the outcome before anyone takes a single bite.
    
    **The** **setup:**
    
    Quick rewind. Madi in the kitchen, talking to camera — something short, competitive, playful. "My kid said the robot's food was better than mine last time. So today we're doing this properly." She's not explaining Posha to the audience (they've seen it in an earlier video). She's just a mom with something to prove.
    
    **The** **cook:**
    
    Both are making the same dish. Madi's cooking her version — she's in her element, she knows this one. Cut to Posha making the same thing.
    
    The robot working, food coming together, steam. Back to Madi plating up carefully, then Posha's plate coming out. Two dishes, side by side. They look similar but not identical. The wife sets them on the table.
    
    **The** **blind** **test:**
    
    Back to the kid at the table, still blindfolded. She reaches for the first plate, takes a bite. Chews slowly, thinking about it, nods.
    
    Reaches for the second plate. Takes a bite. Her face changes — maybe her eyebrows go up, maybe she chews faster, maybe she just smiles.
    
    The audience is reading every micro-expression because the blindfold removes words from the equation. It's all face.
    
    **The** **verdict:**
    
    Blindfold comes off. "Which one?" She points. Madi either celebrates or pretends to be devastated. The wife is laughing either way. The reveal of which plate was Posha's and which was Madi's. The kid takes another bite of her favorite — that's the last shot.
    

### Erika

- Intro Video
    
    **"Making [dish] for my daughter" — @erikarea (Intro Video)**
    
    **Hook (first 2-3 seconds):**
    
    Mom to camera, excited, maybe a little disbelief in her voice: "Can you believe I got a robot chef?" She turns the camera to show Posha on the counter. Quick, casual, the way you'd show a friend something new on FaceTime.
    
    **The setup:**
    
    Mom explains what Posha is and how it works — brief, conversational, not a tutorial. "I'm making [dish] for [daughter's name] today." She picks the recipe, loads the ingredients, gets it going. 
    
    The product introduction happens naturally because she's just showing the audience what she's about to do for her daughter.
    
    **The** **cook:**
    
    Posha working. The food coming together — close-ups, steam, the dish taking shape. Mom nearby, maybe prepping a side or setting the table, but Posha is handling the main dish. She checks on it, reacts to how it looks or smells.
    
    **The taste:**
    
    Daughter arrives or sits down. Mom brings out the food with pride. Daughter takes a bite — and this is the loud moment. A big, genuine reaction. "WAIT. YOU made this?!" or "NO WAY this came from a robot." The disbelief, the volume, the honesty. Mom reveals Posha made it.
    
    Daughter's second reaction — processing that a robot just fed her.
